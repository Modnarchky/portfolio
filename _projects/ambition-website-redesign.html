HEURISTICS EVALUATION

The Current Experience

I conducted a heuristic evaluation using Jakob Nielsen’s 10 usability heuristics to identify the website’s key usability issues and strengths.

Top 3 Problem Areas

Severity 4

Upload a CV leads to a login or register page, confusing users

Severity 4

"Call us" button is a dead end on desktop, plus broken links

Severity 3

Weak search, broken location filter, missing filters



Top 3 Strengths

Strength 1

Errors are surfaced clearly instead of failing silently

Strength 2

Contact methods are instantly recognisable via icons

Strength 3

Filters are reversible and their state is always visible

COMPETITIVE & COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS

Where Do We Stand?

I compared the website to various well-known recruitment firms and job search platforms which are not Ambition’s competitors. Rather than benchmarking only against direct competitors, I looked at established job platforms to identify interaction patterns Ambition could learn from.

The main features in comparison are the filter and sort functions, and the user flow for creating accounts. It was found that Ambition was severely lacking in filters and sort.

USABILITY TESTING

Initial Usability Testing

I conducted 5 usability tests across six key tasks to validate the issues identified. 5 users tested (2F/3M, avg. age 26.8), 4 of whom were new to the site — all working in fields Ambition recruits for. Recurring issues: missing filters, confusing labelling, and a messy, unclear layout — all pointing to the same three things users wanted: simplicity, speed, and transparency.

Task Completion Rate Key Findings
Find a job 4/5 Users struggled to discover and use filters and sorting
Contact a Recruiter 4/5 Users are confused with redundant "Call Us" button and had troubles
Refer a Friend 4/5 Referral actions were difficult to discover
Find Salary Guide 5/5 Users found the information hierarchy and labelling unclear
Upload CV 5/5 Users expected a drag-and-drop interaction and not led to the login page
Account Registration 5/5 Users want more methods of account creation for convenience (E.g. Google)

PERSONAS & PROBLEM STATMENTS

Two Archetypes, Two Shared Frustrations

The Fresh Graduate

Josiah Tan

26 · Software Developer

"Career progression matters the most at this point in time"


Frustrations

  • Unsure of market rate for his skill level

  • Not enough time to prep for technical interview tests


Needs

  • Transparency in salary information

  • Job hunting methods that save time

Mid-Career Jobseeker

Brenda Lim

27 · Senior Accounts Executive

"Salary is the top factor of consideration in a job offer"


Frustrations

  • Has to look through listings job by job — no filtering

  • Similar job titles pay wildly different amounts


Needs

  • A way to benchmark her pay better

  • Clear, concise, more relevant job listings



How Might We...

1. Help Josiah and Brenda each find relevant jobs faster?

2. Give both Josiah and Brenda salary transparency?

Mid-Fidelity Prototype & Testing

I translated the research findings into a mid-fidelity prototype and tested the revised flows with users. Testing focused on whether the new navigation, filtering, and key actions reduced the friction identified in the initial evaluation.
