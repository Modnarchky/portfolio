As sustainability and environmental-friendly sentiments emerge stronger in the modern world, it is worthwhile to celebrate how mankind is a step closer towards fighting climate change and slowing down the damage of human activities on this planet.

Being a nature and animal lover myself thanks to how my parents exposed me to animals, I have always tried to be more environmentally conscious by being mindful of what I use and practising the 3 Rs (Reduce, Reuse, Recycle). Also, my background as an artist does encourage me to be constantly curious about the world which contributes to how I feel about the issue.

Growing up in an Asian household, my mom instilled in me the practice of saving items ‘just in case’ they might be needed in the future. This tendency, common in many Asian families, stems from a historical lack of proper avenues for disposing of items without being wasteful. Admittedly, I’ve inherited a part of this habit from my mom. I find inspiration in the belief that anything can become art, leading me to keep all my school textbooks and schoolwork over the years — items I haven’t touched in over a decade.

As things pile up at home, we have been looking into ways to declutter. Throughout the process, I thought about the impact of art on sustainability and wondered where the line should be drawn.


### Our art may be doing more bad than good
An artist and designer myself, I recognise the importance of art and design in communicating ideas and interacting with the world. As with any pressing issues in the modern world, countless artwork has been by creative individuals worldwide to address their thoughts and expression revolving around environmental issues.


![Plastics piling up like a wave in front of a billboard of a guy surfing](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:720/format:webp/1*TSdAk84kfjGmYqykK_FQQg.jpeg)

However, by creating art with certain used materials, we would be denying the world of resources that could be recycled and made into new objects. Once made into art, sometimes, the material would not be recyclable any more. This results indirectly in a greater production of new materials with new resources. For example, an installation or statue created with plastics held together with glue would render all of the plastics contaminated and thus, unrecyclable. Had these plastics been recycled, a portion of them would be made into new products like water bottles or containers which will then be disseminated back into society. With them being unrecyclable, new plastics will have to be produced to fill that gap.

Being a nature lover, wouldn’t it be counter-intuitive to create art about sustainability but create more waste in doing so? How should I then express my thoughts and share them with others with minimal impact on the environment? Where is the balance between sustainability and art?

### The Selfish Gene and empathy
To answer the questions posed above, it is critical to understand why one would bother caring about the environment. Richard Dawkin’s The Selfish Gene explored selfishness in evolution and this is what he said:

> We are survival machines-robot vehicles blindly programmed to preserve the selfish molecules known as genes.

Being selfish is innate to being human based on how little people care about the environment until the effects of climate change impact us. With climate change taking its toll on humanity, it implores us to think about how it will affect us individually if nothing is done within the next few years. Perhaps, this is the reason why environmental consciousness has been on the rise and it started from a selfish standpoint of sustaining our life in an individualistic manner.

On top of that, while we can’t help being selfish as humans, nobody would be happy to be called “selfish”. As humans are social creatures, we recognise that it would be bad for us if our actions affect others negatively because we risk being outcasted. Also, our emotions play a part in our environmental consciousness. Lastly, while it does not apply to everyone, being empathetic towards wildlife would encourage one to be more aware of how we are damaging the ecosystem with human activities.

In short, some of us care about the environment because

1. We understand the long-term effect on us if we don’t care
2. We hate to be labelled as “selfish”
3. We don’t want to be outcasted
4. We are empathetic and can feel the pain of wildlife affected by man

Out of the four reasons listed above, only one comes from an altruistic standpoint which does not even apply to the whole of mankind. Therefore, I think it is important to recognise and admit that we are a selfish species but still think of ways to reduce our selfish footprints anyway. Today, I have a choice to not reflect on this issue, continuing my creative journey ignorantly by mindlessly creating work. Or I could share my opinion as food for thought for fellow creatives. I choose the latter.


### Working around sustainability for art
As mentioned earlier, art and design are important in communicating ideas and expressing one’s individuality. However, is it worthwhile at the expense of a greater issue? I think we have to ponder and reach an answer ourselves singly. For me, the answer would be to stick to environmentally friendly methods of creating artwork.

Some methods I would consider are:

1. Using strings or biodegradable adhesive to bind materials together when necessary instead of glue
2. Create artwork with organic materials like sand, rocks, leaves etc.
3. Using only unwanted and unrecyclable plastics or materials for art.

For other unwanted materials where the 3R (Reduce, Reuse, Recycle) could be exercised, it would perhaps be best to donate or recycle them appropriately. Donating them to charities would allow them to have a new life while aiding the marginalised communities. Recycling them would help reduce the production of new materials, making sure the used materials are maximised.

All in all, the balance between art and sustainability is a personal decision. We can’t avoid being harmful to the environment but there are steps we can all take to reduce and slow down the negative effects. Compared to the past world in which our parents lived, we are blessed to have multiple outlets to recycle and repurpose materials. Keeping materials for later may not be such a good idea anymore. Sure, it can save some money for us if we happen to need the materials we keep one day. But the percentage and amount we will save is often insignificant. On a larger scale, if everyone were to save and “hoard” materials, it would most definitely reduce the amount of resources we have to share. For instance, the material might become scarce and its price will rise due to a low supply and high demand from a financial point of view.

I am keen to hear how creative individuals of all levels take action to be more environmentally responsible. If you have more ideas on how we can improve the world in this aspect, feel free to connect with me and drop me a message and we can discuss!
