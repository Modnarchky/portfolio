I started building my portfolio since March 2026, because I wanted a website that felt more like me. I did not have a clear roadmap for how I was going to build it and I did not know what I could achieve alone. All I knew was that the portfolio builders I tried weren't giving me the flexibility I wanted, so I decided to see if I could create my portfolio how I wanted it.

Today, the portfolio has grown into something much more than I initially expected. For example, I initially wrote my articles on Medium.com and simply hyperlinked it on my site. Now, I have my own blog system, where I can write, manage and publish my articles directly from the site. Through this journey, I've deepened my knowledge in HTML and CSS, and further explored JavaScript, Jekyll, YAML, Markdown and a little PHP. I've learnt how to troubleshoot problems, fix bugs, deploy a website and designed a back-end content management in ways that make it easier for me to maintain.

But the technical skills aren't actually the most interesting part of the journey. What surprised me most was what I learnt about how I think, how I solve problems, and how I learn. Most importantly, this project is proof that sometimes, the best way to learn is through doing, and the learning process is non-linear.

These are the top 3 lessons that stayed with me throughout the process.

### 1. Our past experiences may be our unexpected teachers
About two decades ago, Blogspot (formally knew as Blogger) rose to fame and almost everyone was using it to create a blogging website. Back then, I wanted to create a blog with my own personality. The process is simple. First, you pick a template to start with, then you adjust the colours by changing the HEX code, or copy chunks from other templates and piece them together to form your own.

I thought I was just building a blog. Little did I know, what I was doing back then was actually coding. Additionally, my field of studies in school were in digital animation and communication design, none of which requires the knowledge of coding. Hence, I thought that I would never use those skills again. 

Who would have thought that two decades later, I would be coding my own website? Even though I had no formal training in coding, I already knew basic HTML and CSS when I starting building my portfolio because the experience from Blogspot stayed with me.

It made me realise that sometimes, the things we learn out of curiosity, without any particular purpose, can come back when we least expect them.

### 2. Knowing when to turn back is a strength
In the early stages of building this website, I didn't have a clear plan for how I wanted to build it. It wasn't because I didn't have a vision for the website. Rather, I didn't know the full extent of what I could achieve. If I had created a plan based only on what I already knew, I would probably have ended up with a rather messy and difficult-to-maintain website.

Hence, I met my first roadblock pretty early in my journey. I knew I wanted to reduce mistakes and the amount of work needed to manage on my portfolio. The very first question I asked was...

> How can I make header.html appear on every page without copying the full code into each page?

With what I knew at that point, I do not know how to resolve it. Then I turned to ChatGPT for guidance. ChatGPT introduced me to PHP which it claimed was better for dynamic content and what I wanted. I researched on the possibility because it was not a simple decision of whether I wanted to write in PHP or HTML. I had to think about how can I put what I coded online and accessible for employers and recruiters. 

It seemed like PHP was the right choice because ChatGPT provided me with solutions on how I could host a PHP website. However, I learnt that it was not so simple through trial and error. The more I explored it, the more complexity I encountered around hosting and deployment. Eventually, I decided to turn back and  revert to HTML, CSS, and JavaScript so that I could host my portfolio on GitHub.

Turning back wasn't giving up. It was choosing a solution that better suited what I actually needed.

### 3. AI can accelerate learning but human judgement and input remain essential
While I used AI to assist me with coding, I learnt that AI's suggestions are still just suggestions. The ultimate decision is still mine to make.

Despite that AI can generate an entire piece of code, I believe it is important to understand the foundations. Otherwise, how would I discern whether the solution it gave me was actually suitable for my needs?

For example, ChatGPT recommended PHP when I was trying to figure out how to make my website more dynamic. It gave me several advantages and disadvantages of the proposed solution. But ultimately, only I could decide whether PHP was the right fit for my portfolio because AI didn't understand all the requirements, constraints and considerations I had for my website.

### Bit by bit, I've built beyond a portfolio
I began this this project with a simple goal of building a portfolio that reflects my professional identity with custom components to showcase my work. I didn't expect it to teach me so much about how I learn and how I approach problems. My experience with Blogspot gave me a foundation I didn't realise I had. Trying PHP taught me that moving backwards can sometimes be the best way to move forward. And working with AI showed me that having access to more information doesn't remove the need for human judgement.

More importantly, I learnt that I don't need to know everything before I start. The journey can be quite frustrating at times, especially during the times where I want to achieve an outcome but I do not know how. But through my frustrations, I asked curious questions and learnt to achieve what I wanted. As lone as I am willing to explore, make mistakes, and change direction when something isn't working, I will make progress and develop my work further.

I am really proud of how far I have came and how my portfolio turned out to be. This portfolio isn't just a collection of my work. It's also a record of how I learnt to build something I didn't think I could build. And hopefully, when someone sees this portfolio, they'll see more than the work I've created. They'll see a designer is curious, resourceful, willing to learn, and capable of turning an idea into something real.
