I’ve had the honour to participate in the Be More Festival 2024 held by ADPList recently. In the session “Unlocking AI: Myths, Facts & First Steps” by Daniel Simons of Human AI Experience, several interesting touch points of Generative AI were discussed. As Daniel shared about the background of Generative AI (GenAI) being a subset of deep learning, I couldn’t help but relate GenAI’s development journey to the rise of calculator usage in our daily lives.

### A father’s teaching which stayed relevant
I recall in my younger days 15 years ago when I first started secondary schools, we were first allowed to use calculators in our school curriculum. It seemed so easy, just a few taps and voila! I’ve got my answer. My father, who is still a passionate private tutor today, had refused to allow me to use the calculator even when the school allowed it. I didn’t understand it then — but now I do. Calculators save time, but without basic math knowledge, how will we understand the process or know if the answer is correct? It will most definitely lead to us becoming blind believers without critical thinking.

### AI can be wrong too
While calculators are accurate today, it’s not the case for our new friend, GenAI. GenAI is still in its early stages, and it has been shown to provide incorrect answers at times.

![ChatGPT saying 'strawberry' has 3 R's](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:720/format:webp/0*TgNMS6HNJW7V2dMh.jpeg)

In Be More Festival 2024, I noticed that many design and technology leaders expressed concern over an over-reliance on AI. The question comes — Where do we draw the line to the usage of AI?

### It’s our responsibility to keep up with the times
Before we explore how we should draw the line, it is important to acknowledge that AI is here to stay despite some negative perceptions against AI from the community. The anxiety of losing our jobs to AI is daunting, I feel it myself too. However, being afraid of what’s to come doesn’t stop it from coming. Instead, what we could do for the benefit of both ourselves and the industry is to actively pursue new knowledge in AI and incorporate it into our professional practice and lifestyle.

The effect of not keeping up with times could be observed even in history, particularly in the decline of Nokia when they refused to move along with new technology and innovation while other competitors had moved to innovate smartphones. History will undoubtedly repeat itself if everyone chooses to be stubborn in their skill set. With that said, it is our onus to make sure we are updated with the industry needs instead of expecting the world to suit us. Time waits for no man.

### AI is not here to replace humanity, hence draw your own line
There are currently no laws and guidelines to dictate how much should we rely on AI. From a designer’s perspective, your top priority is delivering and communicating your clients’ and stakeholders’ messages in projects. If it’s something that is supposed to be human-centric, is it in good taste to use AI-generated collaterals? For example, will the audience feel like visiting a therapist who uses all AI-generated images in their advertisements?
