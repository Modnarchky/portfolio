UX designers pride themselves on empathy — but if we only apply it to user flows and not societal issues, are we really practicing what we preach? Many articles and resources have explored the importance of empathy in UX design. However, I wish to extend the topic and explore why UX designers should be advocating for social causes.

To disclaim, advocating for social causes (sustainability, human rights, animal welfare) isn’t about grand activism. It’s in the small choices: designing inclusively, recycling, donating, or even just talking about issues that matter. It may have been discussed before, but it is important to revisit it — especially as we strive to grow as a community of empathetic designers.

### Empathy: Beyond a Buzzword
What does true empathy look like in practice? To understand this, we first need to break down the definition. Empathy is the ability to step into another’s shoes, share their feelings, and understand their struggles. This core principle drives our work as UX designers — putting ourselves in the shoes of users to design products that solve real problems in their lives.

Unfortunately, in today’s world, the term “empathy” is often overused and even misrepresented. While empathy is a vital trait, it shouldn’t be reduced to a buzzword — it should be a genuine approach to design and life. Teams that care about causes design more inclusively — for example, Duolingo reduces educational barriers for marginalized communities by simplifying language learning. Their empathy for diverse learners reflects a commitment to equitable education.

### Should Empathy End at the Workplace?
Some may argue UX should remain neutral, but design is never value-free — every choice either reinforces or challenges societal norms. Advocating for social causes may not be in a UX designer’s job description, but it’s an essential trait of a good designer. I’d question a designer who claims empathy yet ignores societal issues. How can one claim to empathize with users while ignoring the larger social issues affecting them?

In an age where information is abundant, it’s hard to ignore the existence of worldwide issues that impact humanity on different scales. Genuine advocacy for social causes is a true demonstration of empathy. It’s not just about solving business problems and fix UX pain points — it’s about solving human problems. True empathy drives actions beyond one’s job.

### Empathy Beyond User Flows and Pain Points
When UX designers broaden their empathy to include social causes, they can have a greater impact on the world. Human-centered thinking goes beyond user flows, aesthetics, and business goals — it’s about truly understanding human needs. This expanded view allows us to contribute to causes that align with our values and those of the organizations we work with.

Many businesses and organizations are actively working toward making the world a better place. They need great UX design to help their products and services reach those in need. But only UX designers who share similar values and care about the same causes will be able to truly understand both the user’s pain points and the mission of the product or service.

### UX Design For the Causes I Believe In
This broader empathy isn’t theoretical — it directly influences my personal choices as a consumer and designer. My personal values strongly influences the businesses I apply to (aligned personal values with company values). I care deeply for animal welfare and make it a point to purchase only cruelty-free products for my needs. With Sephora’s additional Clean tab in its navigation bar, I can quickly search for products that align with my values. This feature encourages me to shop on their platform more, and I’m sure it has the same effect on other consumers who share my values.

Another example would be the ALBA Step Up app which I use when donating E-waste. ALBA’s app makes it easy for me to scan the QR Code on the recycle bins. Even with its clunky reviews, the app proves even flawed UX can drive change if it lowers barriers to action.

These are two explicit examples of how UX design plays a role in my decision to use the apps so that I can contribute to the cause I believe in. This highlights the importance of good UX especially for products created for the greater good. To design for these causes, we need teams who genuinely care — which starts with hiring.

### The Hiring Gap: Are We Overlooking True Empathy?
Social issues are seldom talked about in the employment landscape because businesses may be focused on only their business needs, and UX designers need the job for a living, leading to a more technical hiring approach. While technical skills are undoubtedly important, true empathy is even more important. You can train someone how to use Figma, but you can’t teach someone empathy. It has to come from within.

Businesses should realise that distinguishing truly empathetic designers is ultimately beneficial to both parties. Empathetic designers don’t just deliver wireframes. They ask: “Who’s excluded? How does this harm or help?” That’s the mindset that transforms products — and societies. They will question and think about how to bring your product to the next level by putting in hours to create better user experience to retain users. These designers want to make an impact and touch the lives of others through your product.

### Having Conversations is The Way to go
I urge hiring managers and UX designers to bring social issues discussions to the interview ground. When UX designers and hiring managers can discuss social issues in depth and harmony, hiring managers will be able to sift out designers who are true empaths and align with their business goals. For the UX designers, having such conversations will help them feel more passionate about the new role they are given. Ultimately, having conversations will help in distinguishing the intentions of an individual in supporting a cause — is it out of genuineness or for clout?

Even though caring about social issues is what makes our human identity complete, not everyone will truly advocate for something from the bottom of their heart. A UX designer’s impact goes beyond screens and interfaces — having an opinion on social issues can enhance their ability to create truly meaningful experiences.

Hence, let’s make social causes a topic of importance in our design interviews and amidst our lives. Together, we can help foster a community (starting from designers and their employers) that not only thinks about user flows or aesthetics but also makes the world better one conversation at a time.
